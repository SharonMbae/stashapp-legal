What StashApp is

StashApp is a budgeting app for Android. You tell it what you earn, you split that into pockets for the things you need to cover, and it shows you what's safe to spend on everything else.

That's the whole product. It's a planning tool, and it works on the numbers you give it.

What StashApp is not

This matters more than the rest of this document, so it's near the top.

  • We are not a bank, and not a payment service. StashApp never holds, receives, or moves your money. Your money stays in your M-Pesa wallet or your bank account. Nothing you do in StashApp moves a shilling anywhere.
  • We are not connected to your accounts. StashApp has no link to Safaricom, to M-Pesa, or to any bank. It cannot see your real balance, and it cannot make a payment for you.
  • This is not financial advice. Safe-to-Spend is arithmetic on figures you typed in — your income, minus what you set aside. It is not a recommendation, and we are not licensed financial advisers. Decisions about your money are yours.

Who can use StashApp

You need to be 18 or older. StashApp handles money and isn't built for children.

One account per person, and the phone number you sign up with should be one you actually control — it's how you get back in if you forget your PIN.

Your account and your PIN

Your PIN is stored as a bcrypt hash. That's deliberate, and it has a consequence worth being clear about: we cannot recover it and we cannot tell you what it is. If you forget it, you reset it through your phone number. There's no other route in, including through us.

So:

  • Keep your PIN to yourself. Anyone holding your unlocked phone and your PIN can see and change your budget.
  • Five wrong attempts locks the account for 15 minutes. That's protecting you, not punishing you.
  • If you think someone else has your PIN, change it in Settings.

You're responsible for what happens under your account. If you lose access to your phone number as well as your PIN, we may not be able to help you recover the account — there's no identity check we can run that would be both meaningful and safe.

The numbers are yours, and they're what you tell us

Everything in StashApp is self-reported. Your income, your pocket targets, your transactions — you enter them, or you paste in an M-Pesa message and confirm what we read out of it.

We don't check any of it against a bank, a lender, or Safaricom, because we have no way to. Wrong figures in means a wrong Safe-to-Spend out, and the app has no way to notice.

Two things follow from that:

  • Review what the app reads out of a pasted M-Pesa message before you save it. Parsing is a convenience, not a guarantee.
  • For anything that actually matters — tax, a dispute, a loan application — rely on your bank statements and M-Pesa records, not on StashApp.

Premium

Premium is a paid subscription. Some things are worth stating plainly:

  • Google Play handles the payment, not us. The price you see at purchase is the price, in your local currency. We never see your card details.
  • It renews automatically until you cancel, at the interval you chose.
  • Cancel through the Play Store, under Subscriptions — not in StashApp. This is the one people get caught by: uninstalling the app does not cancel your subscription. Only Google can stop the billing.
  • Refunds follow Google's policy. We can't issue them; we have no access to the payment.
  • You keep Premium until the period you paid for ends, including if you cancel on day one.
  • If your payment fails, Google retries for a while. You keep access during that window rather than being cut off mid-retry.
  • If we change what Premium includes, we'll tell you before your next renewal, so you can decide whether to keep it.

Cancelling Premium doesn't delete your data. You keep your account and everything in it; you just lose the paid features.
